# AI and LLM Usage

## Scope

ARCA uses AI/LLM systems as engineering and research-assistance tools.

The project records this from the founding phase so that machine assistance and
human responsibility remain clearly separated.

## Human Origin

The ARCA problem originated from human engineering work:

- high-detail BIM assets are valuable authoritative sources;
- direct BIM publication is often unnecessarily heavy for GIS consumers;
- GIS users need spatially meaningful, queryable, lightweight derivatives;
- renderer lock-in should not become a prerequisite for consuming public
  spatial representations.

Those needs were not generated by an LLM.

## AI-Assisted Work

LLM systems may assist with:

- implementation acceleration;
- debugging and refactoring;
- geometry-algorithm exploration;
- comparison of representation strategies;
- documentation drafting;
- architecture discussion;
- prior-art discovery assistance;
- benchmark design;
- test-case design;
- structured review of failure cases.

## Human Validation

LLM output is not authoritative.

Suggested code, formulas, algorithms, terminology, or documentation may be
accepted, changed, rejected, experimentally tested, or removed.

Human contributors remain responsible for:

- problem definition;
- architecture;
- representation-profile decisions;
- georeferencing behavior;
- algorithm selection;
- validation;
- security;
- licensing and provenance;
- novelty claims;
- benchmark claims;
- stable vs experimental status;
- final release acceptance.

## Authorship

LLM systems are tools.

They are not recognized as founders, authors, copyright holders, maintainers,
project stewards, or accountable decision-makers.

## Working Principle

> **Human-originated, AI-assisted, human-validated.**

## Publication Disclosure

Research publications derived from ARCA should disclose AI use according to the
venue's policy and the actual development/research workflow used for the work.
